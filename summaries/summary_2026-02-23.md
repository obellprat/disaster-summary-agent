## FLOODS AND FLOODING EVENTS

### Indonesia

**Jember Regency Flooding - East Java**
- **Date:** February 12, 2026 (continuing through February 15)
- **Location:** Jember Regency, East Java
- **Cause:** 
High-intensity rainfall caused multiple rivers to overflow, inundating communities across eight sub-districts and 12 villages

- **Impact:** 
  - 
At least 29,800 people affected, including 7,400 families and 557 internally displaced persons; one fatality reported

  - 
Around 7,200 houses affected, and three bridges and several public facilities sustained damage

- **Current Status:** Response operations ongoing
- **Source:** AHA Centre

## VOLCANIC ACTIVITY

### Philippines

**Mount Kanlaon - Negros Island**
- **Alert Level:** 2 (Moderate Level of Volcanic Unrest)
- **Recent Explosive Eruption:** February 19, 2026
- **Details:** 
  - 
A moderately explosive eruption occurred at 4:38 p.m. and lasted for two minutes, generating an eruption plume that rose to 2,000 m (6,600 ft) high and drifted southwest

  - 
Pyroclastic flows were observed descending the southern and southwestern slopes of the volcano

  - 
10 volcanic earthquakes and two episodes of tremor recorded, while sulfur dioxide emissions averaged 346 tonnes per day
 (February 20)
- **Impact:** 
The Department of the Interior and Local Government ordered local governments in Negros Island to enforce strict safety precautions, emphasizing the need to keep danger zones clear

- **Current Status:** 
Mount Kanlaon remained under Alert Level 2 as of February 20, with continued signs of unrest

- **Source:** PHIVOLCS, Global Volcanism Program

**Mount Mayon - Albay Province**
- **Alert Level:** 3
- **Activity:** 
The eruption continued during February 11-18, characterized by lava effusion and collapses at the summit dome, pyroclastic density currents, incandescent rockfalls, ash plumes, and active lava flows

- **Status:** Ongoing eruption
- **Source:** PHIVOLCS, Global Volcanism Program

### Indonesia

**Mount Semeru - East Java**
- **Alert Level:** III (Standby)
- **Activity Period:** February 9-11, 2026
- **Details:**
  - 
The eruption continued with 106 seismically detected explosions reported on February 9

  - 
Ash and gas plumes rose to a maximum of 900-1,000 m (2,953-3,281 ft) above the vent

  - 
Seismically detected rockfalls and flowage events recorded

- **Current Status:** Continuing eruption
- **Source:** PVMBG (Center for Volcanology and Geological Hazard Mitigation)

**Mount Ibu - Halmahera Islands**
- **Alert Level:** 2 (Alert)
- **Activity Date:** February 19, 2026
- **Details:**
  - 
Gas and vapor plumes rose to a maximum of 200 m (656 ft) above the vent, with 104 seismically detected explosions reported

  - 
White-to-gray or gray ash plumes rose 300-600 m above the summit and drifted in multiple directions; incandescence at the summit was visible in nighttime webcam images
 (February 12-18)
- **Public Advisory:** 
The public was advised to stay 2 km away from the active crater and 3.5 km away from the N crater wall opening

- **Current Status:** Continuing eruption
- **Source:** PVMBG

**Mount Marapi**
- **Alert Level:** II
- **Status:** 
Recent volcanic activity reported

- **Source:** PVMBG

## OVERALL REGIONAL CONTEXT


La Niña conditions were predicted to weaken in February 2026 and transition to ENSO-neutral conditions in March 2026, with models predicting ENSO-neutral conditions to persist at least until May 2026
.


For the week following February 15, wetter-than-usual conditions were forecast over parts of western and central Mainland Southeast Asia, as well as across portions of the northwestern and northeastern Maritime Continent
.
