## **FLOODS AND FLASH FLOODS**

### Philippines - Flooding and Landslides from Storm System
- **Date:** February 19-23, 2026
- **Location:** Davao Region and CARAGA Region (Regions XI and CARAGA)
- **Cause:** 
Continuous moderate to heavy rainfall associated with a shear line caused flooding on February 19, 2026, with continued rainfall on February 20 resulting in further flooding and triggering landslides

- **Impact:** 
As of February 23, 2026, around 197,800 families (740,700 persons) were affected across 469 barangays, with around 27,000 families (100,000 persons) in 74 evacuation centers

- **Damage:** 
80 houses were damaged, including 27 totally damaged and 53 partially damaged houses

- **Status:** Response actions undertaken by relevant authorities
- **Source:** AHA Centre Weekly Disaster Update

### Philippines - Tropical Storm Penha (Basyang)
- **Date:** February 4-7, 2026 (dissipated before target period but ongoing impacts)
- **Landfall:** 
Made first landfall in Bayabas, Surigao del Sur on February 4, followed by additional landfalls in Jagna and Dauis in Bohol, Alcoy, Cebu, and Ayungon, Negros Oriental on February 6

- **Impact:** 
Heavy rainfall, flooding, and landslides left 12 dead, 36 injured, and around 28,000 people displaced. A landslide in Cagayan de Oro claimed four lives, while five people died in Iligan

- **Source:** Wikipedia - 2026 Pacific Typhoon Season

## **VOLCANIC ERUPTIONS AND ACTIVITY**

### Philippines - Kanlaon Volcano
- **Date:** February 19 and February 26, 2026 (two separate eruptions)

**February 19 Eruption:**
- **Time:** 4:38 PM local time
- **Type:** Moderately explosive eruption lasting 2 minutes
- **Details:** 
Generated an eruption plume that rose to 2,000 m high and drifted southwest, with pyroclastic flows descending the southern and southwestern slopes

- **Impact:** 
Sulfurous fumes were observed in barangays of La Carlota, Bago, and Moises Padilla, Negros Occidental, with a shock wave heard as a booming sound felt more than 30 km away


**February 26 Eruption:**
- **Time:** 7:04 PM local time
- **Type:** Moderately explosive eruption lasting 2 minutes
- **Details:** 
Generated a plume that rose 2,500 meters above the crater drifting southwest, with incandescent ballistics raining around the crater and pyroclastic density currents descending the eastern and southeastern upper slopes within 2 kilometers of the summit

- **Impact:** 
A loud explosion was heard and slight tremors were felt, with fire seen coming out of the crater
; 
PDCs ignited a forest fire in La Castellana and residents reported ashfall

- **Alert Level:** 2 (Moderate Level of Volcanic Unrest on 0-5 scale)
- **Source:** Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS)

### Philippines - Mayon Volcano
- **Date:** February 23 - March 2, 2026 (ongoing activity)
- **Location:** Bicol region, eastern Philippines
- **Activity:** 
As of February 27, 2026, lava-collapse pyroclastic density current (PDC) was still ongoing, with the volcano sending a fast-moving avalanche of hot gas, ash, and fragmented volcanic rock cascading down its slopes on Friday

- **Monitoring Data:** 
The volcano recorded lava effusion with lava flow on Basud (3.8 kilometers), Bonga (1.6 km), and Mi-isi (1.3 km) gullies, with 378 rockfalls and 20 pyroclastic density current signals

- **Alert Level:** 3 (indicating intensified unrest and relatively high level of volcanic unrest)
- **Restrictions:** 6-kilometer Permanent Danger Zone enforced
- **Source:** PHIVOLCS

### Philippines - Taal Volcano
- **Date:** February 12, 15, and 16, 2026
- **Activity:** 
Three minor phreatic eruptions occurred, each lasting 3-4 minutes, with gas-and-steam emissions generally rising 350-900 m above the crater rim, though on February 16 plumes rose as high as 2.1 km

- **Alert Level:** 1 (on 0-5 scale)
- **Source:** PHIVOLCS

### Indonesia - Mount Ibu
- **Date:** February 19-25, 2026
- **Location:** Maluku Islands
- **Activity:** 
Daily eruptive events recorded with gray ash plumes rising 300-1,000 m above the summit, drifting mainly NE, E, and SE, and occasionally SW

- **Alert Level:** 2 (second lowest on four-level scale)
- **Source:** Pusat Vulkanologi dan Mitigasi Bencana Geologi (PVMBG)
